Current Theme

Human society is like the ocean. Just as the sunlit surface is home to beautiful fish blessed with light and food, human society naturally has an upper class adorned in privilege, wealth, fine attire, and refinement. Meanwhile, deep sea creatures take on unsightly forms just to survive in the dark, food scarce depths; similarly, commoners lack privilege and wealth, prioritizing practical survival over aesthetics. Just as surface and deep sea fish cannot share the same tank, people of different classes cannot coexist. Exceptional beings like the lanternfish may rise from the depths to the surface, but their stay is strictly limited, and they constantly risk becoming prey for surface fish much like Oscar Wilde's fate after infiltrating British high society. Even after tens of thousands of years, humans still build societies remarkably similar to those of our aquatic ancestors. The formation of social hierarchy appears to be an inescapable destiny for humanity.
