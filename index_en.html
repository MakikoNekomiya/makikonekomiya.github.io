Current Theme

Human society is like the sea. Just as the surface is populated by fish adorned with sunlight and abundance, society has its privileged upper classes, dressed in refinement and wealth. In the deep sea, where there is little light and even less food, survive the strange-looking fish that must prioritize usefulness over beauty. In the same way, society also contains the lower classes, who lack privilege and often have little money, and survive by choosing practicality over aesthetics. Just as surface fish and deep-sea fish cannot live in the same tank, different social classes cannot coexist peacefully. There are rare exceptions, like lanternfish that rise from the depths to the surface, but even they have limited time there and face the risk of becoming prey. Comparing this to human society, it resembles the tragic end of Oscar Wilde's entry into the British social world. Even after tens of thousands of years, humans still build societies similar to the ones they had as fish in the sea, and the creation of class seems to be an unavoidable destiny.
